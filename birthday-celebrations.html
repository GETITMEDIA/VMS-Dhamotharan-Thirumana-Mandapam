The Occasion

Room to celebrate without hiring a stadium

Birthday functions are the most varied bookings the hall takes. A first birthday with sixty relatives looks nothing like a sixtieth with three hundred, and a children’s party has a completely different set of needs from either. What they share is that the family wants space without paying for a wedding-scale venue.

The main hall does not have to be used at its full 500-seat capacity. Set out the seating you actually need at one end, keep the cake table and the decorations near the stage, and leave the rest of the polished floor open for children to run about on — indoors, air conditioned, and off the road.

For older guests that same air conditioning is what turns a long afternoon into a comfortable one. The dining hall next door handles the meal separately, so the celebration area is never being cleared while people are still enjoying it.

For the Celebration

Set the hall up the way your day needs

The same facilities as a wedding booking, arranged at whatever scale your celebration actually calls for.

  • Stage for the cake cutting

    A raised stage for the cake table, the decorations and the photographs, with the floor open in front of it.

  • Flexible floor layout

    Use a portion of the hall for a small gathering or the full span for a large one — the seating comes out to suit.

  • Comfortable all afternoon

    Full centralised air conditioning, which matters when a party runs from noon well into the evening.

  • Safe indoor space for kids

    A polished, level indoor floor well away from the road, so children can move around without anyone watching the gate.

  • Separate dining hall

    Meals served in their own hall for 250 at a sitting, keeping food away from the celebration area.

  • Easy parking

    Car and two-wheeler parking on the premises for guests arriving through the afternoon.

Ways to Use the Hall

Two very different birthdays, one venue

01

First birthdays and children’s parties

For a first birthday the family usually wants the ceremony portion — the blessing, the cake, the photographs with each set of relatives — followed by a proper lunch. Keep the seating tight around the stage so the elders are close to the child, and leave the far end of the hall open for the children who have come along. Decorators can work in the hall from the morning, and balloons and backdrops go up against the stage wall.

  • Seating arranged close to the stage for the ceremony
  • Open floor at the far end for children
  • Decoration access from the morning
  • Air-conditioned throughout the afternoon
02

Traditional First Birthday Rituals

A first birthday often involves traditional ceremonies, Ayush Homam, and elders blessing the child with a shawl or gifts. For these functions, the hall works best laid out with seating close to the stage so family can participate in the rituals, and the dining hall opened as soon as the main ceremonies are done. Rooms on site give the family somewhere quiet to rest and change the child's attire between the rituals and the cake cutting.

  • Stage suitable for honouring ceremonies and traditional rituals
  • Unhurried dining for 250 guests per sitting
  • AC rooms on site for the family to rest and change
  • Budget-friendly for smaller gatherings
